\begin{abstract}
Fourier Neural Operators (\fno{}) learn mappings between function spaces with
$O(N\log N)$ cost, but their global mixing channel is \emph{structurally
band-limited}: mode truncation discards every Fourier mode above the cut, so
sharp boundary content and long-range boundary interactions must either be
re-synthesized by pointwise nonlinearities or be lost. We give a precise
statement of this truncation band-limitation, and show that a multiplicative,
field-valued modulation of the spectral weights---derived from a signed
distance function (SDF) of the domain---restores the missing band through
\emph{spectral convolution}: the product of a low-band latent with a kinked
modulation field regenerates high-frequency content at exactly the wall
locations where the truncated operator is wrong. The resulting architecture,
\agfno{}, reduces to an exact \fno{} at initialization (zero-gated), preserving
the standard training dynamics until the gates open. We then do what the
operator-learning literature has consistently failed to do for
``geometric forgetting'': \emph{measure} it. A closed-form linear probe on
each block's latent features shows that decodable boundary information in a
depth-$L$ \fno{} collapses monotonically
($\Rtwo$ $\ProbA$ $\to$ $\ProbB$ over four blocks),
while \agfno{}'s re-injection measurably slows the decay
($\Rtwo$ $\ProbC$ $\to$ $\ProbD$).
On an obstacle-Darcy benchmark with a genuine multigrid-preconditioned
solver as ground truth, the mechanism---not extra parameters, input channels,
or loss reweighting---accounts for the gain: a gates-frozen capacity control
matches the \fno{} baseline (\CtrlFrozenVsFnoRelL{}$\times$ its global error)
while the full mechanism beats its own frozen self by \MechGainRelLRed{} in
global and \MechGainRingRed{} in near-boundary error. Multi-seed
($n{=}3$): \ExpAgfRelL{}$\pm$\ExpAgfRelLStd{} vs.\
\ExpFnoRelL{}$\pm$\ExpFnoRelLStd{} relative $L^2$. The effect compounds with
depth: at 16 blocks the vanilla \fno{} collapses outright
(relative error \DSixteenFnoRelL{}---worse than predicting the mean, with the
probe showing \emph{total} geometric collapse, $\Rtwo = \ProbeExtFnoDSixteen$)
while \agfno{} remains depth-stable (\SweepDSixteenAgfnoRelL{}$\pm$\SweepDSixteenAgfnoRelLStd{} over three seeds,
probe plateaued at $\Rtwo \approx \ProbeExtAgfDSixteen$).
The collapse replicates: a three-seed re-run of the sweep reproduces it with a
$\SweepFnoAgfSixteenRatio\times$ error gap and zero overlap across seeds.
It also lifts: on 3-D obstacle Darcy (spheres and solid tori, multiply
connected, float64 ground truth) the same collapse--plateau pair reappears
at depth 16 (\fno{} \ThreeDDSixteenFnoRelL{} vs.\ \agfno{}
\ThreeDDSixteenAgfnoRelL{}; probe $\Rtwo$ \ThreeDDSixteenFnoProbe{} vs.\
\ThreeDDSixteenAgfnoProbe{}), establishing the phenomenon as
dimension-independent.
We also locate the mechanism's boundary honestly: on a second PDE family
(advection--diffusion past fixed-temperature obstacles) whose difficulty is
not wall-anchored, the same architecture with the same control shows only a
\PtwoMechGainTRed{} effect---a near-null result we report and hypothesize
about, not hide. Code, checkpoints, per-seed results, and one-click GPU
reruns are public.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Neural operators that learn in the frequency domain---most prominently the
Fourier Neural Operator \citep{li2021fno}---achieve speedups of orders of
magnitude over classical solvers while generalizing across discretizations
\citep{brandstetter2022message, takamoto2022pdebench}. Their central
computational primitive, the spectral convolution, applies a learnable
complex weight to the low-frequency modes of the latent field and discards
the rest. The truncation is presented as a benign regularization; the
folklore justification is ``solutions are smooth.''

For problems whose \emph{difficulty lives at the boundary}, the justification
is false. Fluid-thermal boundary layers, wall discontinuities of heterogeneous
media, and shock-adjacent structure are precisely the high-frequency,
geometry-coupled content of the solution. If the operator's only global
mixing channel is band-limited, that content must survive---if it survives at
all---through pointwise nonlinearities acting on low-band inputs, a channel
with no access to where the walls are unless the walls are re-supplied at
every depth.

This article is written as an analysis-and-progress contribution rather than
a benchmark paper: it (i)~\emph{diagnoses} a structural failure mode of
spectral operators, (ii)~supplies a \emph{measurement instrument} (a
controlled linear-geometry probe with null and sanity controls) that makes
the failure quantifiable block by block, (iii)~derives an \emph{a-priori
selection rule} ($\Delta\rho$, computable before any training) that predicts
when the failure matters, and (iv)~validates a minimal \emph{correction}
whose every parameter and loss contribution is individually ablated. Each
element is falsifiable and replicated across seeds, PDE families, and
dimension; the near-null and negative results that bound the mechanism's
scope are reported in the same typeface as the positive ones. A reader
should leave with an explanation of \emph{why} spectral operators fail at
walls they were never shown, a tool to detect it, a rule for when to care,
and a fix --- not with a new state of the art.

This paper makes the mechanism precise and then measures it. Our claims and
contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Theory (Section~\ref{sec:theory}).} A two-line proposition
      states that an \fno{} block's global channel \emph{is} the low-band
      projection: high-band boundary content cannot be created by it, every
      long-range boundary interaction is low-pass filtered through a Dirichlet
      (sinc) reachability kernel, and depth compounds the loss. The
      proposition is elementary; the point is that the limitation is
      \emph{structural}, not statistical.
\item \textbf{Mechanism (Section~\ref{sec:method}).} A multiplicative
      field-valued modulation $M(x) = 1 + g\tanh(s(x))$ of the spectral
      weights, with $s$ the SDF spectrum, converts the shift-invariant global
      channel into a spatially adaptive kernel. Because a product of fields is
      a convolution of spectra, a low-band latent multiplied by a kinked
      modulation \emph{regenerates} the high band---measured here at
      $>20\times$ high-band energy, versus zero for constant modulation. The
      layer is zero-gated, so the network begins as an exact \fno{}.
\item \textbf{Measurement (Section~\ref{sec:probe}).} The ``geometric
      forgetting hypothesis'' has been measured only to depth 4, and only by
      concurrent work \citep{xia2026forgetting}; here it is measured as a
      function of depth to the point of architectural failure. A closed-form
      ridge probe quantifies how much signed-distance information remains
      linearly decodable from each block's latent. In a trained \fno{} it
      collapses $\ProbA \to \ProbB$ (depth $1\!\to\!4$) and then to
      \emph{total} collapse at depth 16 ($\Rtwo = \ProbeExtFnoDSixteen$),
      exactly tracking the operator's behavioural collapse;
      \agfno{}'s injection slows the decay ($\ProbC \to \ProbD$) and then
      holds a plateau ($\Rtwo \approx \ProbeExtAgfDSixteen$ across depths
      4--16). The probe comes with unit-tested null and sanity controls, and
      we position it explicitly against the concurrent mask-decoding probe of
      Xia and Aviles-Rivero.
\item \textbf{Attribution (Section~\ref{sec:controls}).} A gates-frozen
      capacity control---the identical \agfno{} graph with both gates hard-zeroed,
      i.e.\ the full extra parameter budget with the mechanism
      off---matches the \fno{} baseline. The gain is therefore attributable to
      the mechanism, not to parameters (\ExpFnoParams{}M vs.\ \ExpAgfParams{}M),
      input channels (both models receive the same 3-channel input), or the
      boundary penalty (ablated: \PenaltyShareAgfRing{} share of ring error).
\item \textbf{External validity (Section~\ref{sec:external}).} The ordering
      replicates on the canonical piecewise-constant Darcy setting without a
      boundary penalty, and the forgetting story is completed at depths up to
      16: \fno{} collapses outright (relative error \DSixteenFnoRelL{};
      probe $\Rtwo = \ProbeExtFnoDSixteen$) while \agfno{} plateaus
      (\DSixteenAgfnoRelL{}; probe $\Rtwo \approx \ProbeExtAgfDSixteen$,
      spread \ProbeExtAgfPlateauSpread{} across depths 4--16)---confirming
      the proposition's plateau prediction. A Geo-FNO-style
      learned-deformation baseline is included and, as predicted, is blind at
      the walls (ring error \GeofnoRing{} $\approx$ \fno{}'s) while improving
      the global error (\GeofnoRelL{})---the deformation fixes the
      simply-connected part of the problem and cannot touch the walls. A
      second PDE family (advection--diffusion) yields an honest near-null for
      the mechanism, which we analyze: the gain concentrates where difficulty
      is \emph{wall-anchored}. Matched external baselines --- U-Net
      \citep{ronneberger2015unet} and CNO \citep{raonic2024cno},
      width-bisected to the \fno{} parameter budget and trained on
      byte-identical splits under a uniform pilot protocol, with the
      full-budget comparison as the decisive test
      (Section~\ref{sec:baselines-pilot}) --- confirm the near-wall ordering
      is not specific to the Fourier family.
\item \textbf{Prediction (Section~\ref{sec:diagnostic}).} The asymmetry above
      becomes an a-priori prediction. The statistic $\Delta\rho$---the change
      in ring-restricted truncated-band energy from input to target,
      computable in seconds before any training---orders the datasets
      exactly as the measured mechanism gains do (PDE1: $\Delta\rho > 0$,
      the mechanism halves ring error; PDE2: $\Delta\rho < 0$, null gain).
      The naive target-only candidate fails and is documented: only content
      the task must \emph{create} above the cut, not content it inherits,
      predicts mechanism value. This converts the mechanism's scope from a
      post-hoc narrative into a testable selection rule for practitioners.
\item \textbf{Dimension-independence (Section~\ref{sec:3d}).} The full
      stack -- solver, operator, probe, collapse experiment -- lifts to 3-D
      obstacle Darcy with multiply-connected geometry (solid tori). The
      depth-16 collapse of \fno{} and the \agfno{} plateau reproduce
      (Table~\ref{tab:3d}), with the probe separating behaviour from
      representation in 3-D exactly as in 2-D; all pre-registered gates pass.
\end{enumerate}

Everything is reproducible: byte-deterministic data generation, a public
one-click GPU rerun of every experiment, checkpoints, and per-seed JSONs
(Section~\ref{sec:repro}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}\textbf{Neural operators.} \fno{} \citep{li2021fno} introduced learnable
spectral convolutions, anchoring a now-broad operator-learning framework
\citep{kovachki2023nocal}; DeepONet \citep{lu2021deeponet} learns branch--trunk
expansions; graph-based operators \citep{li2021grafno, pfaff2021learning}
handle
unstructured meshes at graph-convolution cost; transformer-style attention
operators \citep{cao2021galerkin, hao2023gnot} and weather emulators built on
them \citep{bi2023graphcast, lam2023learning} substitute global mixing for
grid regularity. \textbf{Geometry
adaptation.} Geo-FNO \citep{mao2023geofno} learns a deformation to a reference
box; GINO \citep{yin2023gino} couples GNN encoders with \fno{} latents;
CNO \citep{raonic2024cno} and related convolutional operators trade the FFT
for multiscale convolutions in the U-Net tradition \citep{ronneberger2015unet}. None of these, to our knowledge, isolates
\emph{why} the spectral operator fails at boundaries---the band-limitation of
its global channel---nor measures the depth-wise decay of geometric
information. Conditioning on geometry (SDFs, level sets) is folklore in
implicit-surface modeling \citep{park2019deepsdf}; the contribution here is not ``condition on
geometry'' but the \emph{band-restoration identity} and its measurement.
\textbf{Over-smoothing and probing.} The reachability-kernel view has an
analogue in GNN over-smoothing analyses \citep{rusch2023survey}; linear probing
is standard methodology \citep{alain2017probing}, applied here to a target
(decodable geometry per depth) that makes a cited hypothesis falsifiable.

\textbf{Geometric forgetting and concurrent work.} The closest work to ours
is Xia and Aviles-Rivero \citep{xia2026forgetting}, who formalize the
geometric forgetting hypothesis via the data-processing inequality, probe it
layer-wise with a mask-reconstruction decoder, and propose architecture-agnostic
\emph{geometry memory injection} (FiLM-style \citep{perez2018film}, additive, or
concatenative) as a remedy. Our work is complementary and, on the mechanism question, takes a
position theirs implies but does not state: additive/FiLM injection changes
the latent but leaves every block's global channel band-limited and
domain-blind (Remark~\ref{rem:injection}), whereas a multiplicative
\emph{field-valued} modulation alters the global channel itself and provably
re-populates the truncated band (Theorem~\ref{thm:restore}). Their probes are
carried to depth 4 on architectural comparisons; we extend the measurement to
depth 16, where the vanilla operator collapses outright and the re-injecting
one plateaus (Section~\ref{sec:deep}), and we decompose error by frequency
band and region rather than reporting decodability alone. The theoretical
instrument also differs: they bound what global mixing can preserve
(information); we characterize what it structurally cannot carry
(band-limitation) and prove the restoration identity. Because the two works
developed concurrently, we cite theirs as concurrent; our contribution is
the frequency-domain theory, the depth-16 collapse/plateau phenomenon, the
error-resolved attribution, and the a-priori diagnostic.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Setup and notation}
An \fno{} block updates a latent $v \in \mathbb{R}^{C\times H\times W}$ as
\begin{equation}
v' \;=\; \sigma\!\big(\underbrace{\mathcal{S}(v)}_{\text{global}}
\;+\; \underbrace{P\,v}_{\text{pointwise}}\big), \qquad
\mathcal{S}(v) \;=\; \mathcal{F}^{-1}\!\big[\, W(k)\,\mathcal{F}v(k)\,\big],
\quad |k_i| \le k_{\max},
\label{eq:fno}
\end{equation}
with $W(k) \in \mathbb{C}^{C\times C}$ learnable per retained mode and $P$ a
$1{\times}1$ convolution. The task: learn
$G^\dagger: (a, \text{geom}) \mapsto u$ on a unit domain
$[0,1]^2$ perforated by $n_{\text{obs}}$ polygonal obstacles
($n_{\text{obs}} \sim \mathcal{U}\{1,5\}$), with
$a$ the (log-)permeability or scalar state, and the SDF
$s(x) \le 0$ inside obstacles. Inputs are the 3-channel stack
$[\,a,\ s,\ \mathbb{1}_{\text{ring}}\,]$ where
$\mathbb{1}_{\text{ring}}$ marks the one-cell band around walls; \fno{} and
\agfno{} receive \emph{identical} inputs. Ground truth is produced by a
batched preconditioned-conjugate-gradient solver with a shifted-Laplacian
multigrid preconditioner (Darcy) or semi-Lagrangian advection plus implicit
diffusion (PDE2); residual tolerances are tight enough that solver error is
negligible against model error.

\subsection{The AGF block}
The \agfno{} block replaces \eqref{eq:fno}'s global channel with
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}_{\text{AGF}}(v)(x) \;=\; \mathcal{F}^{-1}\!\big[
\underbrace{W(k)}_{\text{learnable}}
\cdot \underbrace{\big(1 + g \tanh \hat{s}(k)\big)}_{M(k),\ \text{SDF-derived}}
\cdot \mathcal{F}v(k) \big],
\label{eq:agf}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{s}$ is the spectrum of the input SDF field and
$g \in \mathbb{R}^{C}$ is a learnable per-channel gate initialized at zero,
so \eqref{eq:agf} $\equiv$ \eqref{eq:fno} at initialization (unit-tested:
frozen gates reproduce \fno{} output bit-for-bit at matched weights). A second
anti-forgetting path concatenates the geometry stack
$[\,s,\ \mathbb{1}_{\text{ring}},\ \mathbb{1}_{\text{int}}\,]$ to a pointwise
MLP added to the spectral output, also zero-gated. Total added parameters
vs.\ \fno{}: \FullAgfVsFnoParamsRatio{}$\times$
(\ExpAgfParams{}M vs.\ \ExpFnoParams{}M).

\subsection{Why this restores the truncated band}
Two mechanisms, one dominant. \textbf{(i) Per-pixel re-localization:}
$M$ is a \emph{field}, not a constant; multiplication in frequency space is
convolution in physical space, so the global channel stops being
shift-invariant and its effective kernel can concentrate at walls.
\textbf{(ii) Band restoration (measured):} $s$ has a wall kink, so
$\hat{s}$ decays like $|k|^{-2}$; its direct tail above the cut is small
($\sim 10^{-5}$ of its energy), but the \emph{product} $M \cdot v$ has the
spectrum $\hat{M} * \hat{v}$---a convolution---so even a strictly low-band
$v$ regains high-band content wherever $M$ varies, i.e.\ at walls. Measured
on our geometry: $>20\times$ high-band energy in $M v$ vs.\ $v$; a constant
modulation creates none. Both facts are unit-tested properties of the
architecture, not of a trained model (Section~\ref{sec:theory}).

\section{Theory: truncation band-limitation and three predictions}
\label{sec:theory}

We work on an $N \times N$ grid with the DFT $\mathcal{F}$; write
$P_{\le}$ for the mode-truncation projection implemented by the spectral
convolution ($|k_i| \le k_{\max}$ per axis) and $P_{>}$ for its complement.
A \emph{truncated spectral conv} is $\mathcal{S} = \mathcal{F}^{-1} W
P_{\le} \mathcal{F}$ with learnable complex weights $W(k)$; a \emph{pointwise}
map acts channel-wise at each pixel independently (1$\times$1 convolutions,
MLPs, activations). The proofs are elementary; the point is that they are
statements about the implemented operator, with the code as the model
(supplement \citep{singh2026agfno} mirrors the line-by-line correspondence).

\begin{lemma}[Truncation idempotence]
\label{lem:idem}
$\mathcal{S} = \mathcal{S} \circ P_{\le}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$P_{\le}$ is a projection, so $P_{\le} P_{\le} = P_{\le}$; substituting,
$\mathcal{S} P_{\le} v = \mathcal{F}^{-1} W P_{\le} P_{\le} \mathcal{F} v
= \mathcal{S} v$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[High-band annihilation and sinc reachability]
\label{lem:annihilate}
For every latent $v$: \textup{(i)} $\mathrm{supp}\, \mathcal{F}[\mathcal{S} v]
\subseteq \{|k| \le k_{\max}\}$; \textup{(ii)} a unit perturbation at pixel
$x_w$ reaches pixel $x_q$ through the global channel as
$K_S(x_q - x_w)$, where $K_S = \mathcal{F}^{-1}[W \cdot
\mathbf{1}_{\{|k| \le k_{\max}\}}]$ is a product of Dirichlet (sinc)
factors that depends on the learned weights but not on the domain geometry.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) $\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{S} v] = W P_{\le} \mathcal{F} v$ is supported where
$P_{\le}$ is nonzero. (ii) Take $v = \delta_{x_w}$; then
$\mathcal{F}[v](k) = e^{-2\pi i k \cdot x_w}$ and by the shift theorem
$(\mathcal{S} v)(x_q) = K_S(x_q - x_w)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Harmonic generation]
\label{lem:harmonic}
If $z$ is a non-constant field with $\mathrm{supp}\,\mathcal{F}[z] = B$, then
generically $\mathrm{supp}\,\mathcal{F}[\tanh z]$ strictly contains $B$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Pointwise, $\tanh z = z - \tfrac13 z^3 + O(z^5)$. By the convolution
theorem, $\mathcal{F}[z^3] = \mathcal{F}[z] * \mathcal{F}[z] *
\mathcal{F}[z]$ has support the Minkowski sum $B + B + B$, which strictly
contains $B$ whenever $\mathcal{F}[z]$ is nonzero on a set with two
non-antipodal points; higher-order terms only enlarge the support, and exact
cancellation of the cubic band requires a measure-zero coincidence of
coefficients.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Band-limited global channel]
\label{thm:bandlimit}
In a depth-$L$ \fno{}, \textup{(i)} the high band of any latent state can
only originate in pointwise nonlinearities applied to low-band fields
(harmonic generation, Lemma~\ref{lem:harmonic}), with data-dependent phase
and magnitude; \textup{(ii)} every long-range interaction is transmitted
through the shift-invariant kernel $K_S$ of bandwidth $k_{\max}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilate}), regardless of the PDE's boundary structure.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The latent is assembled from the global channels $\mathcal{S}_l$ and
pointwise maps. By Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilate}(i) no $\mathcal{S}_l$ emits
high-band content, and compositions of band-limited maps remain
band-limited; (i) follows. Statement (ii) is Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilate}(ii)
applied blockwise.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Band restoration]
\label{thm:restore}
Let the AGF global path be $v_{\mathrm{geo}} = \mathcal{S} v \cdot m$ with
$m = 1 + g_s \tanh(z_s)$, where $z_s$ is the geometry spectral conv plus
linear map applied to the SDF channels. Suppose $\mathcal{S} v \not\equiv 0$
and $\mathrm{supp}\,\mathcal{F}[m] \not\subseteq \{|k| \le k_{\max}\}$ (the
latter holds generically by Lemma~\ref{lem:harmonic}, since $z_s$ is
band-limited and $\tanh$ is applied pointwise). Then for every $k$ with
$|k| > k_{\max}$ there exist modes $\kappa$ with
$\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{S} v](\kappa) \, \mathcal{F}[m](k - \kappa)$ as a
nonzero summand of $\mathcal{F}[v_{\mathrm{geo}}](k)$: the high band is
re-populated, at $O(N \log N)$ cost, with content localized at the geometry
since $m$ is a function of the SDF.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Multiplication in physical space is convolution in frequency:
$\mathcal{F}[v_{\mathrm{geo}}](k) = \sum_{\kappa}
\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{S} v](\kappa)\, \mathcal{F}[m](k - \kappa)$. Since
$\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{S} v] \neq 0$ and $\mathcal{F}[m]$ does not vanish on
$\{|k| > k_{\max}\}$, some summand with $|k| > k_{\max}$ is nonzero unless
all such summands cancel -- the same measure-zero coincidence as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:harmonic}. Cost: $z_s$ requires one FFT and one inverse FFT
plus a $1 \times 1$ convolution; the Hadamard product is $O(N^2)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Necessity of field-valued modulation]
If $m$ is constant -- in particular at initialization, where the gate $g_s = 0$
-- then $\mathcal{F}[v_{\mathrm{geo}}]$ has exactly zero energy above the
cut. Band restoration is caused by the modulation being a \emph{field}, not
a scalar; it is measured at $> 20 \times$ high-band gain for the learned
field versus exactly zero for a constant
(test\ \texttt{test\_agf\_modulation\_restores\_high\_band\_content}).
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Why modulation, not injection]
\label{rem:injection}
Additive geometry injection ($v + W_m g$) and FiLM affine modulation change
the latent but leave every block's global channel exactly as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:bandlimit}: band-limited, sinc-shaped, geometry-blind;
geometry added to the latent is annihilated by the next truncation in the
global path (Lemma~\ref{lem:idem}) and re-enters only through pointwise
paths. Multiplicative field modulation is the minimal change that alters the
global channel itself, turning the shift-invariant kernel into a
geometry-adaptive one and provably re-populating the truncated band
(Theorem~\ref{thm:restore}) before the next block's truncation. This is the
precise structural delta between \agfno{} and injection-style remedies.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Aperture decay is empirical, not structural]
\label{prop:bandlimit}
Theorem~\ref{thm:bandlimit} bounds what the global channel \emph{can} carry;
whether decodable boundary content in fact decays with depth depends on the
learned weight concentration (the ``effective aperture''). This is an
empirical quantity, and we measure it (Section~\ref{sec:probe}):
\fno{}'s decodable geometry falls monotonically to total collapse at depth
16 while \agfno{}'s plateaus -- the aperture prediction P3.
\end{proposition}

The proposition is deliberately modest; its value is converting a folklore
explanation (``spectral bias'' \citep{rahaman2019spectral, rahaman2019cnn,
basri2020frequency, wang2022freq}) into a structural statement with three
falsifiable consequences:

\begin{description}
\item[P1 (Propagation).] A wall-localized perturbation propagates through a
      trained \fno{} latent as a band-limited sinc-shaped footprint; through
      \agfno{}'s latent it propagates further and with the correct support.
      \emph{Measured} by per-band error on perturb-vs-clean pairs
      (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}).
\item[P2 (Spectral signature).] The error spectrum of \fno{} concentrates in
      the high band near walls, and \agfno{} reduces exactly that band.
      \emph{Measured} by band $\times$ region error decomposition.
\item[P3 (Probe plateau).] If forgetting is the aperture decay of
      Proposition~\ref{prop:bandlimit}(c), the probe $\Rtwo$ of a re-injecting
      architecture should \emph{flatten} at depth while \fno{}'s keeps
      falling. \emph{Measured} at depths up to 16 (Section~\ref{sec:probe}).
\end{description}

The predictions are also ordered by scope. P1--P2 are statements about
\emph{where} the mechanism acts (the truncated band, near walls); P3 is a
statement about \emph{when} depth helps. Section~\ref{sec:diagnostic} adds
the practitioner-facing consequence: a statistic of the dataset alone that
predicts whether the mechanism will pay, verified across the two PDE
families here and falsifiable on any future one.

\section{Experimental design}
\label{sec:design}

All experiments share byte-identical data (seed-pinned generators, verified
by local regeneration matching remote outputs to 4 decimals), identical
budgets across variants, and identical input channels across architectures.
The controlled matrix on obstacle Darcy (res $48^2$; 1{,}024/128/512
train/val/test unless noted):

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\fno{}}: baseline, Eq.~\eqref{eq:fno}.
\item \textbf{\agfno{}}: Eq.~\eqref{eq:agf} + anti-forgetting path, gates live.
\item \textbf{\agfno{}-frozen}: identical graph and parameter count, gates
      hard-zeroed (capacity control).
\item \textbf{\agfno{}-spec-only}: spectral gate live, anti-forgetting MLP
      frozen (component ablation).
\item \textbf{No-penalty} pair: both models trained without the boundary
      penalty (loss ablation).
\item \textbf{Deform-\fno{}}: Geo-FNO-style learned deformation
      (identity at init, unit-tested) + standard \fno{} on the warped frame.
\end{itemize}

Metrics: global relative $L^2$; near-boundary relative $L^2$ restricted to
the ring band; wall fidelity---mean $|$pred$-$target$|$ inside obstacle
interiors (target is the exact Dirichlet value, so the metric is absolute,
not relative); and for PDE2, zero-shot rollout error at $2T$.
Three seeds for headline pairs; mean $\pm$ std reported.

\subsection{An a-priori diagnostic: predicting where the mechanism pays}
\label{sec:diagnostic}

The scope statement above was made post hoc. Can it be made \emph{before}
training? Theorem~\ref{thm:bandlimit} suggests the mechanism's value tracks
the truncated-band content the task forces the network to \emph{create} --
not the truncated-band content it must merely preserve. This distinction
carves the diagnostic into shape empirically. Define

\begin{equation}
\rho_{\mathrm{ring}}(f) \;=\; \frac{\sum_{|k| > k_{\max}} |\widehat{f \cdot
r}(k)|^2}{\sum_{k \neq 0} |\widehat{f \cdot r}(k)|^2},
\qquad
\Delta\rho \;=\; \rho_{\mathrm{ring}}(u) - \rho_{\mathrm{ring}}(a),
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}

where $r$ is the near-wall ring mask, $a$ the input field, $u$ the target,
and $k_{\max}$ the architecture's mode cut. Our \emph{first} candidate --
target $\rho$ alone -- failed, and the failure is documented: PDE2's targets
carry \emph{more} truncated-band energy than PDE1's
($\rho_{\mathrm{ring}}$ \RhoPdeTwoRing{} vs.\ \RhoPdeOneRing), because its
initial conditions are already cut at the walls while PDE1's input is a
smooth random field. Target $\rho$ orders the two datasets exactly opposite
to the measured gains. The corrected statistic $\Delta\rho$ measures what
the operator must \emph{create}:

\begin{table}[t]
\centering\small
\caption{The a-priori diagnostic vs.\ the measured outcome. All $\rho$
values are computed from raw fields before any training
(Equation~\ref{eq:rho}); gains are the mechanism-vs-frozen-control ring
ratios from the experiment suites ($<1$ = mechanism helps). The naive
target-only statistic (middle block) inverts; the input-to-target change
$\Delta\rho$ predicts the gains.}
\label{tab:diagnostic}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
Dataset & $\rho_{\mathrm{ring}}(a)$ & $\rho_{\mathrm{ring}}(u)$ &
$\Delta\rho$ & gain (ring) @ $T$ \\
\midrule
Obstacle Darcy (PDE1) & \RhoInPdeOneRing & \RhoPdeOneRing &
\DRhoPdeOne & \DiagGainPdeOneRing \\
Advection--diffusion (PDE2) & \RhoInPdeTwoRing & \RhoPdeTwoRing &
\DRhoPdeTwo & \DiagGainPdeTwoRing \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:diagnostic} shows the result. The two PDE families have
$\Delta\rho$ of nearly equal magnitude with opposite sign --- PDE1's solve
\emph{creates} wall-anchored truncated-band content ($\Delta\rho =
\DRhoPdeOne$), PDE2's dynamics \emph{inherit and smooth} it
($\Delta\rho = \DRhoPdeTwo$) --- and the measured mechanism gains order
exactly with that sign: a \DiagGainPdeOneRingRed{} ring-error improvement on
PDE1, a null \DiagGainPdeTwoRingRed{} on PDE2. The reading is mechanistic,
not merely correlational: the band-limitation theorem says the global
channel cannot carry what must be created above the cut; the AGF modulation
is exactly the restoration term (Theorem~\ref{thm:restore}); so mechanism
value should appear precisely where the task creates truncated-band
boundary content --- which is what Table~\ref{tab:diagnostic} observes.
Two caveats: with two families this demonstrates the diagnostic's
\emph{form}, not a validated selection rule (a panel of datasets would;
the released code computes $\Delta\rho$ in seconds per dataset); and
$\Delta\rho$ is a first-order proxy --- it measures created sharpness, not
whether the created content is decision-relevant for the task's loss.
Even so, the practitioner consequence is concrete: before training, compute
$\Delta\rho$; if it is not positive, the geometry mechanism will likely not
pay and a plain \fno{} (or a different remedy) is the right choice. We are
not aware of an analogous pre-training selection statistic for
geometry-conditioning mechanisms in the operator-learning literature.

\subsection{Three dimensions: the collapse--plateau replication in 3-D}
\label{sec:3d}

Every result above lives in 2-D. The mechanism claims, however, are about
the operator's band structure and the Markovian decay of geometric
information -- neither of which knows about dimension. We therefore lifted
the full stack to 3-D obstacle Darcy: the unit cube with random heterogeneous
permeability and 1--2 analytic obstacles per sample, now spheres \emph{and}
solid tori (the torus keeps the domain multiply-connected in 3-D, where the
deformation-based escape is structurally unavailable), exact Dirichlet

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}

Every experiment is a self-contained kernel with the full package embedded;
each reruns from a clean container with zero internet access and produces the
checkpoint set, metrics JSONs, and figures consumed by this paper. Data are
byte-deterministic: local regeneration reproduces the remote splits exactly,
and local re-evaluation of the pulled checkpoints matches the remote metrics
to four decimals. Public artifacts: training/checkpoint repository
(\texttt{huggingface.co/Sejibeji/agfno-darcy}), and one-click reruns of the
headline, controlled-suite, benchmark, extended, and 3-D replication
experiments (\texttt{kaggle.com/code/sehajrsingh/agfno-darcy-*}).

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

(i) Two PDE families; the geometry suite spans polygonal obstacle fields in
2-D and spheres and solid tori in 3-D, but airfoil-class geometries and
time-varying boundary motion remain untested. (ii) Published numbers are
quoted for scale with explicit non-comparability caveats; external
validation comes from U-Net and CNO reimplemented at matched settings on
our splits (Section~\ref{sec:baselines-pilot}\ifdefined\UNetRelL{}
and Section~\ref{sec:baselines}\fi;
the pilot establishes the four-way ordering at a uniformly reduced
protocol, the full-budget comparison is the decisive one) --- faithful
re-implementations, not the authors' original code, and no external
\emph{operator} baseline beyond the convolutional and deformation families
is included. (iii) The probe measures \emph{linear}
decodability---a lower bound on retained geometric information; the
mitigation-vs-elimination distinction at depth 16 relies on it.
(iv) Obstacles act as fixed-temperature (PDE2) or sealed (Darcy)
inclusions; dynamic boundary motion is out of scope. (v) Single-family
velocity field (smooth random stream function) for PDE2.
(vi) The $\Delta\rho$ diagnostic is validated on the two PDE families it
was constructed to explain; it demonstrates functional form, not a
validated selection rule, until tested on further datasets
(Section~\ref{sec:diagnostic}).

\section{Conclusion}

Geometric forgetting in spectral operators is not mysterious; it is the
band-limitation of their only global channel, stated in two lines and
measurable in one. A zero-gated SDF modulation restores the truncated band
by spectral convolution, converts the shift-invariant kernel into a
wall-localized one, and delivers a controlled, seed-replicated,
cross-PDE-validated error reduction whose attribution survives capacity,
component, and loss ablations. The deeper contribution is methodological:
the hypothesis that operators forget geometry has circulated as folklore;
here it acquires a number, a control, and a falsifiable plateau prediction.
